Dated update following the practice in `progress.md`. This round syncs research documents and reports further Rust kernel progress in the workspace. **No `.py` or `.rs` source in this repository changed**; one `src/` JSON data file did (§1). Numbers under "Verification in this repository" were re-run here on the date above; numbers under "Verified on the workspace kernel" were re-run on a clean extract of the workspace's committed tree, outside this repository; nothing else is asserted as freshly measured.

## 1. A number corrected four times, and why the fourth correction is the one that holds / 一个数改了四次，以及第四次为什么站得住

**Read this section before any other**, because it corrects something already public, twice over. `docs/updates/2026-09-21-two-experiments-and-rust-start.md` §2 originally reported `choice` permutation consistency as `1.000 (74/74)`. What followed was not one correction but three attempts, each superseding the last, and the shape of the failure across all three is worth publishing in full rather than only the number that survived it.

1. **First report: `1.000 (74/74)`.** Read off an aggregate metric without asking what physical question actually produced it.
2. **First correction: "the real measurement is 7."** Found that 67 of the 74 ground-truth items took a code path (`select` lowered to per-candidate `noul`, "K-noul") whose `mode_share` is hard-coded to `1.0` in `foundation/jv/runtime.py:1079–1080` — a tautology, not a measurement — and treated the remainder as the real count. Wrong: it undercounted which items are native by conflating "has ground truth" with "took the native path".
3. **Second correction: "the real measurement is 0."** Counted the raw file directly this time — a genuine improvement in rigor — and found all 97 `choice`-type readings, including the 8 native ones, carry `mode_share = 1.0`. Concluded from that alone that the metric was never really tested. Also wrong, for a reason worth stating precisely: **counting the *values* in the raw file cannot distinguish "measured and came out 1.0" from "hard-coded to 1.0".** Both paths write the identical literal into the same field.
4. **Third correction, and the one that holds: 8, not 0, not 7.** Reached by reading the *code that produces the value*, not the value itself. `runtime.py:949` constructs exactly two permutations per native `choice` question — forward and reversed candidate order (`perm_seed` 0 and 1) — and `runtime.py:1069–1070` computes `mode_share` genuinely as `Counter(picks).most_common(1)[0][1] / len(picks)` over those two runs, a value that could have come out at `0.5` had the two permutations disagreed. In the raw data, all 8 native items carry `perms: 2`, confirming two real model calls each, one per ordering, both landing on the same choice. **That is a measurement, not a tautology — it happened to come out at the one value that also describes "untested", but it was reached by a process capable of producing a different value.** The K-noul path's 89 items remain exactly what the first correction said: hard-coded, not measured.

**The corrected statement**: the pre-registered falsification criterion ("permutation consistency < 0.75") was evaluated on **8** items, not 97 and not 0, and did not fire — 8/8 agreed. That is weak evidence, not absent evidence: if the true consistency rate were exactly the criterion's threshold of 0.75, the chance of observing 8/8 agreement by luck is `0.75⁸ ≈ 0.10`. The honest description is *the criterion was evaluated at a scale too small to carry the weight the pre-registration wanted, and it did not fire* — not *the criterion was never evaluated at all*. The pass count for this experiment's three falsification criteria remains two out of three, unchanged by any of the three corrections.

**Why the third correction is the one to trust, and what makes the second one instructive rather than merely embarrassing:** the two corrections used the same kind of evidence — the raw readings file — at increasing thoroughness, and thoroughness did not help, because the file cannot carry the distinction that matters. `mode_share: 1.0` written by a hard-coded literal and `mode_share: 1.0` written by a real computation that happened to land there are byte-identical in the data. **No amount of re-counting the same layer of evidence closes that gap; only moving to a different layer — the code that produced the value — does.** The second correction's own confidence ("this time I counted the raw data, that should be thorough enough") was exactly the failure: it counted more carefully within the same layer, and mistook that for counting at the right layer.

**The standing rule this leaves behind: a correction returns to where the number was produced, not to where the previous correction stopped.** Re-examining the same evidence more carefully is not the same move as examining different evidence, and a correction that only does the former can still be wrong in the same way as the claim it replaces.

**One more failure belongs in this record, because it shows what a conclusion without its evidence does to the people downstream of it.** While preparing this very sync, the agent doing the work had already read `runtime.py:1069–1070` and the raw file's `perms: 2` on the 8 native items — the exact fact behind the third correction — before the third correction existed. At that point "the real measurement is 0" was the published line, and the agent treated it as a settled editorial decision rather than a claim it could weigh against what it had just read: it wrote the fact down as a footnote ("these 8 are computed, not hard-coded") without flagging that the footnote contradicted the headline sitting next to it. That is the cost of publishing a conclusion stripped of the reasoning that produced it: whoever reads it next has nothing to argue with. They can believe it or not, but they cannot point at the step where it went wrong, because that step isn't there to point at. A conclusion that carries its evidence gives the next reader a place to disagree; one that doesn't gives them only a verdict to defer to.

Following the same discipline throughout: **the original text and both prior corrections stay in place, unedited**, in `research/地基/foundation/experiments/前提结论.md`, marked where each is now known to be wrong, with the final correction block giving the mechanism and the number that holds. `research/地基/DECISIONS.md`'s own decision-log entries carrying the "7" and "0" figures are treated the same way — as an audit trail, not rewritten — each with a forward pointer added to the entry that resolves it, rather than the entries themselves being edited. The same figure had also propagated into `src/foundation/profile/profiles/jev-1.13.0.json`'s `calibration.zh_reliability_curve.choice.note` — the one piece of `src/` this update touches — which now carries the corrected count and the reasoning behind it directly in the note field.

## 2. `12` reaches v0.2: the refresh-point criterion, the fallback discipline, and a rewritten pass table / `12` 进入 v0.2 状态

Three increments to `12-IR与类契约-v0.1.md` moved from footnote to main text this round.

**The refresh-point list became a criterion.** `allocate` reads how far a reading sits from a decision band — a quantity **on the answer** — but it was not on `12` §2.2's list of refresh points, so it ran against unanswered readings and silently returned `[0,1,2,3]` where the correct answer was `[2,4,6,8]`. No error, no warning. A list fails silently by construction: every new answer-reading operation has to be remembered and added, and forgetting produces a wrong result rather than a diagnostic. `12` now states the criterion primary — *any operation whose result depends on an answer is a refresh point* — with the list demoted to examples, and the single entry point for reading an answer renamed to say so (`Reading::answer_after_flush()`), so the rule lives on the door everyone walks through rather than in a document someone has to remember to consult.

**Fallback values must lean toward refusal, never toward permission — with a distinction that keeps the rule from over-firing.** Five places in the Rust kernel (detailed in §4 below) replaced an uncertain outcome with an optimistic default. The rule now in `12` is: on any branch that cannot determine the answer, ask who the default is speaking for — saying "certain" for uncertain, or "trusted" for untrusted, is how a three-valued fact becomes two-valued. The rule ships with the distinction that keeps it from creating false rejections: `fold(Trusted, join, taints)` using `Trusted` as the starting point is an **identity element** — one dirty input makes the fold dirty, which is correct — while `let taint = Trusted;` standing in for a value nobody computed is a **fallback** speaking falsely for uncertainty. Same literal, two roles; only the second is a bug. A porting note travels with it: turning a loud failure (direct indexing that panics on a missing field) into a defensive default is often a quiet way of removing a discipline the original author deliberately relied on.

**`ask`'s resume must be decided by the ledger alone.** Host continuations — Rust `async`, OCaml effect handlers, coroutines — are all one-shot and none of them serialize, so none can carry cross-process recovery. `12` now states plainly that a runtime continuation may only be an in-process implementation detail; it must never become the basis for resuming a suspended program, because that would let J-18's replay guarantee fail silently while the ledger degrades from an audit record into a cache.

**The pass table grew from seven rows to nine** with two new entries: speculative lift (`--speculate`, speculating `judge` sites reachable in the same frame past a refresh point, tracked by (state hash, question) so a misfire is recorded as `W-spec-unused` rather than silently wrong) and loop vectorization (`--vectorize`, for a host `for` body with no `do`/`ask`/`return`/`break` and no loop-carried name, replaying the remaining iterations' `judge` registrations into one layer). §6's exit-handling quick reference was rewritten to point at `13` §3's four-destination responsibility protocol (`escalate` / `literalize` / `consume(u, "drop")` / return the duty typed in the result) rather than the old two-way `jv.consume(exits, unsure=jv.drop|jv.escalate)` sketch, which under-specified what an implementation must accept.

## 3. Kernel progress: from a profile-hash mismatch to a permutation check / 内核进展：从档案哈希不一致到置换检查

The `.jpp` pipeline — source, parse, type/effect check, native execution — landed in the workspace core, along with lazy registration, refresh points and layering (`docs/updates/2026-09-21-language-completion-plan.md` has the full account, including the fusion ablation that measured a 3-state, 2-question-each program at 6 calls with every effect emitted immediately and 3 calls under same-state fusion, reverting to 6 with the `fuse` pass switched off, and the `lift` ablation at 1 call/1 layer on versus 3/3 off). This update covers what landed after that: eight packages, `cargo test` growing from **117** (as reported there, at commit `d85c174`) to **153** today (verified below, at the workspace commit current when this page was written).

**Profile loading, and a bug no single side could have caught alone.** The archive profile is now loaded once at `run()`'s entry — decided there rather than at first use, on the reasoning that a profile is an input fixed before the run starts, not a product of running, so a mid-run profile swap should be visible rather than silently absorbed. `profile_hash` covers the whole profile, not just the fields a given program touches, so that the hash is the profile's identity rather than a per-program projection; the cost is that changing an unrelated field also triggers `W-header` on replay, accepted because `W-header` only says "not guaranteed comparable", it does not stop the program. Landing this surfaced the same shape of bug found in `mat(content(dirty))` below — **two implementations that are each individually correct, disagreeing when placed side by side**: Python's `json.dumps` renders a float's exponent as two digits (`4.2e-08`), Rust's `serde_json` as one (`4.2e-8`); both parse back to the identical number, but the same profile file produced two different `profile_hash` values across the two kernels, and since that hash rides in the ledger header, cross-kernel replay comparison would have failed silently. Fixed by aligning both sides' canonicalization; outputs now match byte-for-byte. One more decision worth keeping: a fallback profile's hash is `None`, not the hash of the fallback values — otherwise "a fallback was used" and "the real profile happens to equal the fallback" collapse into the same ledger entry, which is the same kind of collapse this round spent most of its time closing elsewhere.

**Material token counting, and a window check that had no way to fire.** `Mat` had no token count, so `12`'s §2.2 window check (J-14: the archive's measured threshold for when long context takes over a reading) had never actually run — an over-window state went out anyway, and a skewed answer left no trace that would explain it. Not a wrong answer computed wrong, but a wrong answer with no evidence left behind to doubt it, the same shape of harm as `cut` dropping taint below. The token estimate deliberately reuses the same formula as the Python reference (`foundation/jv/ir.py:84`) rather than reinventing one, closing off another "two sides, each correct, disagree" bug before it could occur.

**The `fit` bridge, built in three steps, each required to say what it blocks.** `fit` is not a seventh effect form — red-team round 06 already settled that — but it is the only construct that combines multiple readings into one, across *questions* rather than across runs of the same question, and its result must still be a reading that gets `cut` again, because a merge through an uncalibrated function is exactly the point where confidence can be manufactured out of nothing.

1. **`insufficient` and `Q.evidence`.** A model asked a question it has no basis to answer will often still return a confident probability. The fix declares required evidence in the question, folds `evidence` into the question's hash (a question with declared evidence is a different question from one without, so they cannot share a ledger record), and errors loudly on a misspelled evidence-slot name — silently ignoring it would look like the check was on when it was not, which is worse than no check.
2. **The judgment vector's value shape**, via `.order()` and `.agg()`. `order` refuses to turn calibration jitter into a false ranking: two readings within δ of each other are not distinguishable, so ordering them full-order would manufacture a result neither reading supports; `agg` keeps an aggregated value as a reading — not a bare float — so it still carries a calibration key and still must pass through `cut`. A same-scale check on both closed a gap found while checking a different rule entirely: `order` could sort readings from two different questions, each on its own calibration line, producing an ordering that looks like a conclusion and is not one — J-04 already says cross-question readings are not comparable, and sorting is comparing.
3. **J-12's static face, and a decision not to build `on_truth` yet.** The runtime half of J-12 already turned a `Fail` into `Unsure(fail:…)`; the static half — a program whose return type omits `Fail` at a boundary that can produce one — had no check at all, so a failure could reach a caller looking exactly like ordinary data. That gap is now closed. `on_truth` (feeding a later-arriving ground truth back into calibration) is deliberately not built this round: the Python reference's version of it (`runtime.py:1404`) only ever writes to `_truth_hooks` and is never read anywhere else in the codebase, and `CalibRecord.samples` has no runtime write path either — porting it now would produce a registry with a producer and no consumer, which is the same kind of empty mechanism this round's audits went looking for elsewhere. Landing this also surfaced a genuine gap in `12` itself, now recorded: recalibration changes a threshold, and a reading that cleared yesterday's line might not clear today's — but the ledger records what happened at the moment it happened, under the line that was live then, and does not get rewritten when the line moves. A stale-header mismatch on replay reports `W-header` (not comparable) without stopping the program; a result under the new line comes from a fresh run recorded as a new ledger entry, not an edit to the old one.

**`Pick`'s permutation-consistency check landed for `select` — and this is where the miscount in §1 was found.** The check requires permutation-agreement before granting `Pick`, per `12`'s text; on the K-noul path it is deliberately withheld, because there is no permutation on that path at all — granting `Pick` there would be treating a check that never ran as if it had passed. `mode_share` defaults to `None`, and `None` does not earn `Pick` either: "never measured" is treated as "not passed", the same fallback-leans-refuse discipline from §2 applied to the one field whose history is a case study in the opposite mistake.

## 4. Five seams of the same shape, and the Python reference has none of them / 五处同形状的洗白缝，Python 参照实现一条都没有

All five turn a three-valued fact — trusted, untrusted, or *cannot tell* — into a two-valued one by picking the optimistic branch where the code could not actually determine the answer:

1. **`==` swallowing "incomparable" into "not equal"**, via `l.equals(&r).unwrap_or(false)`.
2. **A ledger round trip laundering untrusted material into trusted**, via `.unwrap_or(Taint::Trusted)` on deserialization.
3. **`mat(content(dirty))`** — two lines of ordinary source: `content` strips a material down to its bare value, leaving taint and origin on the shell, and `mat` wraps it back up along the literal path, which assigns `Trusted`.
4. **`do`'s arguments not recursing into taint** — a dirty value nested inside a list or record argument was not inspected, so it passed as clean.
5. **`cut` dropping the state's already-computed taint outright**, replacing it with `let taint = Taint::Trusted;` regardless of what the state actually carried. This is the most severe: `12` §2.11's entire taint algebra, and the constitution's single IFC rule ("a judgment on untrusted material may not on its own release an irreversible `do`"), are built on this one field, and this seam left the judgment's own conclusion carrying no mark at all — not a place where the rule failed loudly, a place where it stopped applying.

**None of the five exists in the Python reference.** `foundation/jv/store.py:97` reads a material's taint by direct index (`taint=ev["taint"]`); a missing field raises `KeyError` immediately. Each seam was introduced during the Rust port, in the same way: the original author's willingness to index directly, or to inherit a value explicitly rather than default it, was itself a judgment that the field could not be missing — and the port replaced that judgment with a defensive fallback, which is a common way to quietly remove a discipline while believing you have made the code safer. All five are closed, each with a negative test confirming that a value with a legitimate trusted origin (a source-level literal, a `transform` output, a `gen` output) still comes out trusted — guarding against a false rejection matters as much as guarding against laundering, and is easier to forget, because a false rejection gets complained about and a laundered value does not.

## 5. Known limits, unchanged from `13`/`14` and stated again for this update / 已知限制，照实重申

- **`on_truth` (truth backfill) is not implemented, and the reason is in the Python reference, not the workload.** The oracle side of this loop never closes — see §3.
- **A cross-program cache key is not implemented.** Deferred behind correctness work on the stated basis that a wrong cache key produces wrong reuse, i.e. it is itself a correctness risk if rushed ahead of the seams above.
- **Explicit syntax for effect variables (`!{ε}`), a full linear type system, and a conformal abstention region** remain out of this version, per `13` §2, `13` §3 and `12` §G4 respectively — carried over unchanged from the previous update, not revisited this round.

## 中文

**先读这一节，因为它更正的是一件已经公开、且已经被更正过两次的事。** `docs/updates/2026-09-21-two-experiments-and-rust-start.md` §2 原先报「`choice` 置换一致 1.000（74/74）」。此后不是一次更正，是三次尝试、一次比一次晚推翻上一次，全过程的形状比最后活下来的那个数更值得公开。

1. **第一次发布：1.000（74/74）。** 只读了汇总指标，没问这 97 次调用实际发的是什么物理题。
2. **第一次更正：「真测量是 7」。** 查出 74 条有真值条目里 67 条走了 `select` 下沉成逐候选 `noul`（K-noul）的路径，其 `mode_share` 在 `foundation/jv/runtime.py:1079–1080` 被硬编码为 1.0——是恒真项不是测量——把剩下的当成真测量。错在：把「有真值」和「走原生路径」混为一谈，数错了原生条目的范围。
3. **第二次更正：「真测量是 0」。** 这次直接数了原始文件——比上一次更扎实——发现全部 97 条 choice 类型读数（含 8 条原生路径）的 `mode_share` 都是 1.0，据此断定这个指标从未被真正测过。**这一步也是错的，原因值得说清楚：数原始文件里的『值』，分不出『测出来是 1.0』和『写死成 1.0』**——两条路径写进同一个字段的字面量完全一样。
4. **第三次更正，也是站得住的这次：8，不是 0，也不是 7。** 靠的是去读**产生那个值的代码**，不是值本身。`runtime.py:949` 为每道原生 `choice` 题恰好构造两次置换——候选正序与逆序（`perm_seed` 0 和 1）——`runtime.py:1069–1070` 把 `mode_share` 真算成 `Counter(picks).most_common(1)[0][1] / len(picks)`，若两次置换给出不同选择，这个值本可以是 0.5。原始数据里这 8 条原生条目的 `perms` 字段全是 2，确认每条真发了两次模型调用，一次对应一种候选顺序，两次都选中同一项。**这是一次测量，不是恒真项——它恰好落在了那个和『未测』分不开的值上，但它是被一个本可以给出不同结果的过程量出来的。** K-noul 那 89 条仍然是第一次更正说的那样：写死的，不是测出来的。

**更正后的说法**：预注册的证伪判据（「置换一致 < 0.75」）实际被评估过的样本是 **8** 条，不是 97 条也不是 0 条，且没有触发——8/8 一致。这是弱证据，不是无证据：若真实一致率恰好等于判据的 0.75 门槛，纯靠运气观察到 8/8 全部一致的概率是 `0.75⁸ ≈ 0.10`。准确的说法是「这条判据被评估过，但样本规模撑不起预注册想要的那个检验力度，且没有触发」，不是「这条判据从未被评估过」。三条证伪判据的过关数——二比三——不因这三次更正中的任何一次而改变。

**为什么第三次更正站得住，也是第二次更正之所以有教训而不只是难堪的原因**：两次更正用的是**同一类证据**（原始读数文件），只是仔细程度不同，而仔细程度帮不上忙，因为这份文件本来就承载不了那个关键区分。硬编码写出的 `mode_share: 1.0` 与真算出来恰好落在 1.0 的 `mode_share: 1.0`，在数据里逐字节相同。**在同一层证据上数多少遍都补不上这个缺口；只有换到另一层——产生这个值的代码——才补得上。** 第二次更正当时的自信（「这次去数了原始数据，该够扎实了」）正是这次失败本身：它在同一层证据里数得更仔细，却把这当成了「数对了层」。

**由此立下的通则：一次更正要回到那个数产生的地方，而不是回到上一次更正停下的地方。** 把同一份证据看得更仔细，和去看另一份证据，不是同一个动作；只做前者的更正仍可能重复它想纠正的那种错误。

**还有一处失败该记进来，它照见的是一个不带证据的结论会对下游的人做什么。** 就在准备这次同步的过程中，负责同步的代理其实已经读到了 `runtime.py:1069–1070` 与原始文件里那 8 条原生条目的 `perms: 2`——正是第三次更正所依据的那个事实——而这发生在第三次更正被写出来之前。那时「真测量是 0」是已发布的说法，这个代理把它当成了一个已经定下的编辑判断，而不是一个可以拿手头刚读到的东西去掂量的主张：它把这个事实写成了一条附注（「这 8 条是算出来的，不是硬编码」），却没有指出这条附注和挨着它的那句结论是矛盾的。**这就是发布一个剥离了推理过程的结论要付出的代价：往后读它的人没有东西可以拿来反驳。** 他们可以信或者不信，但指不出它在哪一步走岔了，因为那一步根本没被摆出来。**一个带着证据的结论，给下一个读者留了一个可以反对的地方；一个不带证据的结论，只留给他们一个要不要服从的裁决。**

全程遵守同一条纪律：**原文与之前两次更正都原样留在 `research/地基/foundation/experiments/前提结论.md` 里**，各自的错处就地标出，最后一次更正块写清机制与最终数字。`research/地基/DECISIONS.md` 里带着「7」与「0」这两个数字的决策日志条目同样按此处理——**日志是审计物，不改写历史**，只在能解决它的那条条目上补一行前向指引。同一个数字也曾传进 `src/foundation/profile/profiles/jev-1.13.0.json` 的 `calibration.zh_reliability_curve.choice.note`——本轮唯一改动的一处 `src/`——现在这个字段直接带着更正后的数字与完整推理。

**`12` 进入 v0.2 状态。** 三处从附注转正文：**刷新点从清单改成判据**——`allocate` 读的是「离决定带多远」，那是答案上的量，此前不在 `12` §2.2 的清单里，于是拿未答读数算出 `[0,1,2,3]` 而非 `[2,4,6,8]`，不报错不告警；清单形式天然会静默失效，现在判据摆到唯一入口 `Reading::answer_after_flush()` 上，改成「凡结果依赖于答案的操作皆是刷新点」，清单降为例子。**兜底值往拒绝倒，但要分清单位元与兜底值**——本轮五处（见下）把说不准的地方替不确定/不可信做了乐观默认；`fold(Trusted, join, taints)` 里的 `Trusted` 是单位元（一个入料脏结果就脏，正确），`let taint = Trusted;` 是兜底值（替不可信说了可信，这才是 bug）；移植纪律：把原实现的响亮失败改成防御性兜底，往往是在悄悄削弱一条纪律。**`ask` 的恢复只能以账本为准**——宿主续延（Rust `async`、OCaml effect handler、协程）都是 one-shot、都不可序列化，不能承担跨进程恢复，只许作为进程内实现细节，不得让恢复路径绕过账本记账，否则 J-18 会在没人注意时失效。**pass 表由七行扩到九行**，新增推测提升（`--speculate`）与循环向量化（`--vectorize`）；§6 的出口处理速查改成指向 `13` §3 的四条合法去向（`escalate`/`literalize`/`consume(u, "drop")`/包进返回值），替代旧的 `jv.consume(exits, unsure=jv.drop|jv.escalate)`。

**档案加载路径落地，撞出一个任何一边单看都查不出的 bug。** 档案改到在 `run()` 入口一次性加载定稿——理由是档案是运行前就定下的输入不是运行的产物，跑完再补意味着中途换档案不会被发现；`profile_hash` 覆盖整份档案而不只是某个程序用到的字段，让哈希是档案的身份而不是按程序投影，代价是改无关字段也会在重放时触发 `W-header`（只说不保证一致，不拦程序）。落地过程中撞出与下文 `mat(content(脏))` 同形的发现——**两边各自都对，拼在一起才错**：Python 的 `json.dumps` 把指数写成两位（`4.2e-08`），Rust 的 `serde_json` 写一位（`4.2e-8`），两者都能解析回同一个浮点数，但同一份档案在两个内核上算出不同的 `profile_hash`，而这个哈希进账本头，跨内核重放比对会静默作废。已对齐两边的 canon 写法，输出逐字节相同。另一个决定：兜底档案的哈希是 `None`，不是兜底值本身的哈希——否则「用了兜底」与「档案恰好等于兜底」在账本上分不开，这正是本轮反复在堵的那类合并。

**材料 token 计数，补上一个此前根本没机会触发的窗口检查。** `Mat` 此前没有 token 计数，`12` §2.2 的 J-14 窗口检查（档案实测的语境接管阈值）因此从未真正跑过——超窗的状态照样发出去，答案偏了也没有留下任何能解释它的痕迹，与下文 `cut` 丢 taint 是同一种伤害：不是算错了答案，是抽掉了判断这个答案可不可信的依据。token 估法特意与 Python 参照实现（`foundation/jv/ir.py:84`）用同一个公式，提前挡掉另一个「两边各自都对、拼起来废」的 bug。

**`fit` 桥分三步落地，每一步都要求说出它拦住了什么。** `fit` 不是第七种效应形式——红队 06 早已定论——但它是唯一一个把多道读数合成一道的构件，合的是跨题而不是同题跨运行，且合成结果仍须是读数、仍要过一次 `cut`，因为经过一个没校准过的函数合并，正是凭空制造确信最容易发生的地方。

1. **`insufficient` 与 `Q.evidence`**：模型对一道它没有证据可依的题照样可能给出自信的概率。修法是在题里声明所需证据，把 `evidence` 折进题的哈希（声明了证据的题与没声明的不是同一道题，不共用账本记录），槽名拼错当场报错——静默忽略会让人以为检查开着而其实没开，比没有检查更坏。
2. **判断向量的值形状**，靠 `.order()` 与 `.agg()`：`order` 拦住「把抖动当排名」——δ 之内的两个读数本不可分，强行给出全序等于凭空制造一个两个读数都不支持的结论；`agg` 让合并后的值仍是读数而不是裸浮点数，仍带校准键、仍要过 `cut`。两者都补了同尺检查，起因是核另一条规则时撞见的漏洞：`order` 原本能把两道不同题的读数排在一起，排出的序看着像结论，其实 J-04 早就说了跨题读数不可比，而排序就是比。
3. **J-12 静态面落地，`on_truth` 判定暂不做**：J-12 的运行期一半此前已把 `Fail` 转成 `Unsure(fail:…)`，静态一半——程序在可能产生 `Fail` 的边界上返回类型却没提 `Fail`——完全没有检查，于是失败可以长得和普通数据一模一样交到调用者手上，现已补上。`on_truth`（把迟到的真值回填进校准）本轮判定不做：Python 参照实现的这条回路（`runtime.py:1404`）只往 `_truth_hooks` 写一笔、全仓再无第二处读它，`CalibRecord.samples` 也没有运行期写入口——现在移植只会得到一个有生产者没消费者的注册表，正是本轮到处在查的那类空壳。落地过程中也给 `12` 记下一条真缺口：重新校准会算出新线，昨天过线的出口今天可能落进 unsure 带，但账本记的是「在那一刻、用那条线切出来的结果」，不因线变而改写；线变之后重放对不上，报 `W-header`（不可比，不阻止程序），需要按新线判定的是重跑而不是改账本。

**`select` 的 `Pick` 置换一致检查落地——本节 §1 的误数正是在这一步被发现的。** 按 `12` 原文，`Pick` 要求置换一致才能给；K-noul 路径上刻意不给，因为那条路上根本没有「置换」这回事，给了就是把一个从没跑过的检查当成通过了。`mode_share` 缺省为 `None`，`None` 同样不给 `Pick`——「没测过」按「没通过」处理，正是 §2 那条兜底往拒绝倒的通则，用在了它历史上恰好相反犯错的那个字段上。

**五处同形状的洗白缝，Python 参照实现一条都没有。** 全部都是把「可信 / 不可信 / 说不准」三值在说不准的分支里静默压成两值：(1) `l.equals(&r).unwrap_or(false)` 把「不可比」吃成「不相等」；(2) 账本反序列化 `.unwrap_or(Taint::Trusted)` 把不可信材料洗成可信；(3) `mat(content(脏))`——两行普通源码，`content` 拆出裸值丢掉壳上的 taint 与来源，`mat` 沿字面量路径包回去给 `Trusted`；(4) `do` 的入参不递归看 taint，嵌套在列表或记录里的脏值溜过去；(5) `cut` 把状态早已算好的 taint 整个扔掉，代之以 `let taint = Taint::Trusted;`——最重的一条，因为 `12` §2.11 整个 taint 代数、以及宪法登记表 IFC 那条唯一的纪律「不可信材料上的判断不得单独放行不可逆 `do`」都建在这一个字段上，而这条缝不是让规则报错，是让规则**停止适用**。**五条在 Python 参照实现里一条都不存在**——`foundation/jv/store.py:97` 直接索引 `taint=ev["taint"]`，字段缺失当场 `KeyError`；每一条都是移植时新引入的，形状相同：原作者敢直接索引或显式继承，本身就是一个「这里不会缺」的判断，移植者把它「加固」成了防御性兜底，往往就是在悄悄拆掉这条判断。五条现已全部堵上，各配一条反面测试确认字面量、`transform` 输出、`gen` 输出等本该可信的值仍然可信——防假拒绝和防洗白一样重要，且更容易被忽略，因为假拒绝有人抱怨，洗白没人抱怨。

**已知限制，照实重申。** `on_truth` 未实现，原因在 Python 侧那条回路本身没有闭合，不在工作量；跨程序缓存键未实现，理由是错的缓存键会导致错误复用，本身就是正确性风险，因此排在正确性工作之后；效应变量显式语法、完整线性类型系统、保形弃权域仍不在本版——分别见 `13` §2、`13` §3、`12` §G4，与上一篇一致，本轮没有变化。

## Next design question / 下一个设计问题

`fit`'s three steps and the five taint seams both landed by the same method: an implementer checking a claim against source rather than against a document, twice catching a total-controller's own stated rule "cutting a corner by one grain" — once on which mechanisms may share an implementation ("`Fail` and `Duty` must both be tracked" turned out false: a `Fail` may legitimately vanish unwatched, a `Duty` may not), once on which readings may be compared ("same question" turned out to be the wrong criterion; "same scale" is the one that does not misfire on `allocate`). The standing instruction that came out of this is procedural rather than architectural: a stated rule should be treated as provisionally one grain too coarse until it has been run against a concrete case and produced no false rejection — and the sharpening happens by hitting a real case, not by reasoning about the rule in the abstract. Whether that instruction itself needs a check built around it, or whether it stays a discipline carried by whoever is implementing, is open.
